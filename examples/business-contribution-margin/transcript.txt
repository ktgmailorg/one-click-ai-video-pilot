NARRATOR: Imagine a product sells for one hundred dollars and requires sixty dollars of

variable cost for each unit.

NARRATOR: The forty-dollar difference is its unit contribution margin.

NARRATOR: It is not automatically profit.

NARRATOR: It is the amount that one additional sale contributes toward fixed costs and, after

those are covered, operating profit.

NARRATOR: Divide forty dollars by the one-hundred-dollar selling price and the

contribution margin ratio is forty percent.

NARRATOR: That means forty cents of each sales dollar is available for fixed costs and then

profit, under the model's assumptions.

NARRATOR: Ratios make it easier to compare products with different prices.

NARRATOR: Managers use contribution margin to explore break-even volume, product mix, and

the effect of changing price or variable cost.

NARRATOR: The result is only as good as the assumptions.

NARRATOR: Costs may change outside a relevant range, capacity may be limited, and demand may

respond to price.

NARRATOR: Contribution margin is a decision model, not a substitute for context.
