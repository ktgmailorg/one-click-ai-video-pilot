NARRATOR: An interdisciplinary plan should not begin by collecting unrelated courses that

sound interesting.

NARRATOR: Begin with an outcome. NARRATOR: What problem do you want to address, for whom,

and what would a successful result look like? NARRATOR: A concrete outcome provides a reason

for bringing multiple fields together.

NARRATOR: Next, assign each discipline a job.

NARRATOR: One field might provide technical methods, another might explain human context,

and a third might support design or evaluation.

NARRATOR: The connection must be explicit.

NARRATOR: If removing a field would not change the question or method, its role in the plan

may not yet be clear.

NARRATOR: Finally, sequence the learning.

NARRATOR: Identify foundations, methods, advanced work, and a project that forces the

fields to interact.

NARRATOR: RIT individualized study pairs this planning with academic advising and

connections to faculty and experiential learning.

NARRATOR: The goal is not maximum variety.

NARRATOR: It is an intentional path that produces evidence you can address the

question.
