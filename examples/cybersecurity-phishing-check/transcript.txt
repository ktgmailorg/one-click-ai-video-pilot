A suspicious message tries to collapse the time between reading and acting.

Pause.

Look for urgency, emotional pressure, an unexpected attachment, a request for private

information, or a sender address that only resembles the real organization.

One red flag is not proof, but it is a reason to stop.

Do not use the message itself to prove the message is legitimate.

Instead, open the organization's known website, use a saved phone number, or start a

fresh message to a trusted contact.

Check the full destination and sender carefully.

Independent verification breaks the attacker's control of the path.

If the message remains suspicious, report it using your organization's approved process or

your email provider's phishing control.

Then delete it without opening links or attachments.

Reporting helps defenders protect other recipients.

The safe sequence is pause, verify independently, report, and remove.
