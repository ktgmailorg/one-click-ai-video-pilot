NARRATOR: Choose a point at x equals a and a second point h units away.

NARRATOR: The line through them is a secant line.

NARRATOR: Its slope divides the change in function value by the change in input.

NARRATOR: That number describes average change across the interval, not yet what happens at

one exact point.

NARRATOR: Now shrink h without setting it equal to zero.

NARRATOR: As the second point approaches the first, the secant lines approach a limiting

position: the tangent line.

NARRATOR: If their slopes approach one finite value, that value is the derivative f prime of

a.

NARRATOR: The limit turns nearby average change into instantaneous change.

NARRATOR: A positive derivative means the function is increasing locally; a negative

derivative means it is decreasing; and a zero derivative gives a horizontal tangent.

NARRATOR: Close to the point, the tangent line can approximate the curve.

NARRATOR: The derivative is therefore both a geometric slope and a practical rate of

change.
