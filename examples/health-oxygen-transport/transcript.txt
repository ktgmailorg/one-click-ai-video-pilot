NARRATOR: In the lungs, inhaled air reaches tiny alveoli beside pulmonary capillaries.

NARRATOR: Oxygen crosses the thin respiratory membrane because its partial pressure is

higher in alveolar air than in the arriving blood.

NARRATOR: Diffusion continues toward equilibrium; the membrane does not actively

pump oxygen into the bloodstream.

NARRATOR: Only a small amount of oxygen remains dissolved in plasma.

NARRATOR: Most enters red blood cells and binds reversibly to hemoglobin.

NARRATOR: This transport system greatly increases how much oxygen the blood can carry.

NARRATOR: Oxygenated blood then returns to the heart and is pumped through the systemic

circulation.

NARRATOR: Active tissues consume oxygen during cellular respiration, lowering oxygen partial

pressure around their cells.

NARRATOR: That gradient favors oxygen leaving the blood, separating from hemoglobin, and

diffusing into tissue.

NARRATOR: The complete path is driven by linked gradients: ventilation refreshes

alveolar air, circulation moves blood, and metabolism maintains demand.
