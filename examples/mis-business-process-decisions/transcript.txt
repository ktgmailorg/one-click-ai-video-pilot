An information system is not just an application.

Imagine a customer places an order.

A person confirms the request, a process defines what should happen next, data records

the facts, and technology moves that record to the people who fulfill and monitor the order.

A useful system coordinates all four parts around a business outcome.

The order becomes useful only when its data is trustworthy.

Required fields are validated, identifiers connect related records, and business rules

prevent impossible states.

The same reliable record can support inventory, billing, customer service, and

fulfillment without each team inventing a different version of what happened.

Managers do not need every database row.

They need a decision view: are orders late, where do exceptions occur, and which change

would improve the outcome?

A dashboard summarizes evidence, but people still interpret it in context.

Their decision can revise staffing, inventory rules, or the process itself.

That feedback loop is the purpose of MIS: use information to improve how the organization

works.
