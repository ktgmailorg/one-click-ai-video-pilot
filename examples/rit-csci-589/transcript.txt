Begin by framing Introduction to Thesis Preparation as a question about inputs,

decisions, and observable results.

Students work with a supervising faculty member to complete their undergraduate thesis

proposal, and do additional background preparation (for example, programming, study,

exercises, and analysis) for the subject area and specific problem(s) to be addressed in a

thesis.

Consider this worked case: A student collaborates with their advisor to draft a

proposal that outlines a machine learning project on educational data analysis.

List the givens before choosing a method, because an unstated assumption can change the

conclusion.

Now define Defining the Research Problem precisely enough that another learner could

test the definition.

A research project narrows a question, reviews literature, defines a reproducible method,

evaluates results, and communicates for the intended audience.

question about 'bias detection in automated grading tools.

Separate the formal definition from an implementation detail, then identify what

evidence would distinguish them.

A precise definition makes later examples easier to evaluate and revise.

Examine the tradeoffs in Results and Limitations, including what improves and what

becomes more difficult.

Results should be reported accurately rather than selected to create a misleading

conclusion.

Consider this worked case: Students draft a subsection explicitly stating potential biases

in their dataset and how they will validate their findings.

State the benefit, the cost, and the condition under which the tradeoff is acceptable.

Making the tradeoff visible prevents a local improvement from hiding a larger cost.

Stress-test the reasoning around Research Integrity and Plagiarism with an edge case and

identify which assumption controls the outcome.

Authors should preserve research integrity and avoid plagiarism, fabrication, and

falsification.

Consider this worked case: Students use citation management tools to track all

references and verify that all code and data are properly attributed.

Change one assumption at a time and observe which part of the reasoning no longer holds.

The edge case reveals the boundary of the claim without discarding the useful core idea.

Locate the first point where observed behavior diverges from expected behavior, then test the

Finish with Practical Analysis Checklist as a practical checklist that can guide a new

analysis.

At the end of the semester, a thesis proposal must be submitted for approval to the advisor.

Consider this worked case: Students run a final review against a checklist: problem

statement, literature review, reproducible methods, ethical considerations, citations,

and advisor deliverables.

For each checklist item, record the evidence, the remaining uncertainty, and the next

verification step.

The result is a repeatable procedure, not a conclusion that depends on memory or

intuition.
