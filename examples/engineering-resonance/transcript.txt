NARRATOR: Every oscillating mechanical system has ways it naturally prefers to move.

NARRATOR: Push a swing slowly, or far too quickly, and the motion stays modest.

NARRATOR: Push near its natural rhythm and each input can reinforce the motion already

underway.

NARRATOR: That frequency-sensitive amplification is the core idea behind

resonance.

NARRATOR: A frequency-response plot puts driving frequency on the horizontal axis and

response amplitude on the vertical axis.

NARRATOR: The peak marks the resonant region.

NARRATOR: Its location and width tell engineers which excitations are most dangerous

or most useful.

NARRATOR: A design does not merely ask whether a force exists; it asks how often that force

repeats.

NARRATOR: Real systems lose energy through damping, so their response does not grow

without bound.

NARRATOR: Engineers can add damping, change stiffness or mass, or keep operating speeds

away from a troublesome natural frequency.

NARRATOR: The practical lesson is simple: resonance is not automatically failure, but

ignoring the frequency relationship can turn a small repeating force into a large motion.
