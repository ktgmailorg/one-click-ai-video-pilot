NARRATOR: Exposure is not one brightness dial.

NARRATOR: It is the combined result of aperture, shutter speed, and ISO.

NARRATOR: If one setting admits or records less light, another may need to compensate.

NARRATOR: The useful question is not only whether the image is bright enough, but what

visual trade-off each setting creates.

NARRATOR: A wider aperture admits more light and can produce shallower depth of field.

NARRATOR: A narrower aperture admits less light and can keep more of the scene in focus.

NARRATOR: A fast shutter can freeze motion; a slow shutter records motion across time.

NARRATOR: These are creative decisions as well as exposure decisions.

NARRATOR: When depth of field and motion already determine aperture and shutter speed,

ISO can help reach a usable brightness.

NARRATOR: Raising it can also make noise more visible and reduce image quality.

NARRATOR: Start from the look the photograph needs, then balance the three controls instead

of treating any one setting as automatically correct.
